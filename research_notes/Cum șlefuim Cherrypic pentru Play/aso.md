# Google Play ASO for Cherrypic (photo cleaner / organizer / AI editor), 2025–2026

Research method note: WebFetch could not resolve support.google.com, play.google.com, apptweak.com, mobileaction.co or appgoblin.info from this session (they are outside the allowed network domains). Findings from those sites come from search-result summaries, not full-page reads. developer.android.com was fetched in full. No keyword-volume tool (AppTweak, Asolytics, AppDrift) was reachable, so keyword volume and difficulty figures are mostly missing (see Gaps).

## 1. High-intent keywords (EN, RO, DE/IT/ES/FR) and competition

### Takeaway
English "photo cleaner" plus "swipe" is a very crowded niche on Play: dozens of near-identical "Photo Cleaner: Swipe …" titles, and the leaders have only a few thousand ratings. "Duplicate photos" is the older, established intent. Cherrypic's distinct angles ("AI photo culler / best shot picker", "on-device / private", "organizer + editor") look less crowded, but no volume data could be verified for them.

### Cited Findings
- Many Play apps use almost the same "Photo Cleaner + Swipe" title pattern: "Photo Swipe - Photo Cleaner", "Photo Cleaner & Photo Swipe", "Swipe to Delete: Photo Cleaner", "Photo Cleaner: Swipewipe", "Photo Cleaner: Swipe & Delete", "Swipe Photo Cleaner - Clean AI", "Swipe Photo Storage Cleaner", "Photo Delete Swipe Cleaner", "Photo Cleaner: PhotoSwipe", "Photo Cleaner: Swipe Right", "Swipe Photo Cleaner: Swipelit" — [AppGoblin search results](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.afternoonproducts.swipewipe/keywords); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.ftg.photo.swipe); [AppGoblin](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.eco.photocleaner)
- Size of these apps (third-party tracker data via search summary): Swipewipe about 4.41★ from about 5.2K ratings, updated March 2026. "Swipe Photo Cleaner - Clean AI" (HEYOS) 5.7K+ installs and 227 ratings at 4.42. "Photo Cleaner: Swipe & Delete" (Silveroot) about 79.1K installs, 107 ratings, 3.0★ — [AppGoblin Swipewipe](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.afternoonproducts.swipewipe); [AppGoblin Clean AI](https://appgoblin.info/apps/co.appnation.phonecleaner); [AppGoblin Swipe & Delete](https://appgoblin.info/apps/com.photo.cleaner.swipe2delete)

"Appolyze: Duplicate Cleaner" (April 2026) markets that it does not upload the gallery to the cloud. - German search language seen in editorial content: "doppelte Fotos löschen", "Fotos aufräumen", "Duplikate finden", "ähnliche Fotos" — [Vodafone DE](https://www.vodafone.de/featured/article/doppelte-fotos-loeschen-in-android-so-gehts-219221); [techzeitgeist](https://www.techzeitgeist.de/smartphone-fotos-aufraeumen-duplikate-finden-loeschen-und-automatisch-sortieren/); [mwm.ai DE listing "Photo Cleaner - Smart Cleaner": "erkennt und entfernt mühelos doppelte und ähnliche Fotos"](https://mwm.ai/de/apps/app/6757508537)
- Romanian: the only RO editorial source found is old. Mobilissimo (2015) describes Gallery Doctor as removing "fotografiile nedorite" (unwanted photos), with similar or poor-quality detection and swipe to keep or delete — [Mobilissimo](https://www.mobilissimo.ro/aplicatii-telefoane/aplicatii-utile-gallery-doctor-aplicatia-ce-ne-ajuta-sa-eliminam-fotografiile-nedorite-din-telefon)
- Google Play exposes no keyword volume. Tools estimate popularity, and AppDrift scores difficulty 0–100 from the top-10 apps' reviews, ratings, keyword-in-title share and publisher diversity. Guides advise new apps to target difficulty under about 30 — [App Radar](https://appradar.com/academy/research-new-app-keywords); [AppDrift](https://appdrift.co/tools/keyword-difficulty); [AppFollow 2026](https://appfollow.io/blog/google-play-aso-keywords)

### Inferences
- The candidate keyword sets below are built from competitor titles and editorial language plus translation. They have not been checked against any volume tool.
  - EN core: photo cleaner, gallery cleaner, duplicate photos, similar photos, blurry photos, delete photos, swipe photos, clean up photos, free up storage, photo organizer, best photo picker / best shot, AI photo culling, photo editor (very competitive).
  - EN differentiators: "AI culler", "best shot picker", "burst photo picker", "on-device / offline / private", "no cloud upload".
  - RO: curățare galerie, curăță galeria, poze duplicate, poze similare, poze neclare, șterge poze, organizare poze, eliberează spațiu, editor foto. Also plan for diacritic-less variants such as "curatare galerie" and "poze duplicate": Romanian users often type without diacritics. That is an inference; check that Play's search matches both forms.
  - DE: Fotos aufräumen, doppelte Fotos löschen, ähnliche Fotos, Galerie aufräumen, Speicher freigeben, Foto-Organizer, Fotos sortieren.
  - IT: pulizia galleria, foto duplicate, foto simili, libera spazio, organizza foto.
  - ES: limpiar galería, fotos duplicadas, fotos similares, liberar espacio, organizar fotos.
  - FR: nettoyeur photos, photos en double, photos similaires, libérer de l'espace, trier photos.
- Because "Photo Cleaner: Swipe…" is saturated, Cherrypic should probably lead with a distinct brand plus one high-intent term, e.g. "Cherrypic: AI Photo Cleaner" (27 chars) or "Cherrypic – Best Shot Picker". The short description and long description can then cover duplicate, swipe, organize, edit, on-device.
- Privacy/on-device is a credible differentiator for EU users, but competitors (Appolyze, Shred) already use it, so pair it with the culling ("picks your best shot") angle.

### Gaps

- No reliable source on Romanian Play search behaviour (diacritics, RO vs EN query share).
- IT/ES/FR keyword lists are translations, not observed data.

## 2. Title, short description, long description patterns and policy

### Takeaway
Limits are 30 / 80 / 4,000 characters. The title carries the most weight. Sources disagree on whether the short description is indexed; most say it carries some weight but its main job is conversion. Google's metadata policy bans ranking/performance claims ("#1", "Best of Play", "Popular", award icons), emojis or repeated special characters in the title, icon and developer name, and keyword stuffing.

### Cited Findings
- Title 30, short description 80, full description 4,000 characters. The title is the most important text field — [DEV Community 2025 guide](https://dev.to/iris1031/app-store-optimization-guide-how-to-rank-higher-on-ios-and-google-play-in-2025-1p98); [AppTweak Google Play checklist](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/app-store-optimization-aso-checklist-for-google-play)
- Short description indexing is contested. MobileAction says keywords in it "do give a ranking boost", though conversion is its main purpose. Another guide says it is "NOT indexed". A middle view recommends 1–2 supporting keywords with clarity first — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/blog/google-play/google-play-short-description-keywords); [tessl ASO skill (weak source)](https://tessl.io/registry/skills/github/Eronred/aso-skills/android-aso/review); [AppTweak keyword research](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/play-store-keyword-research)
- Long description: put primary terms in the opening two or three sentences and avoid keyword stuffing — [Sonar: long description keyword structure](https://trysonar.app/blog/google-play-long-description-keyword-structure.md)
- Google metadata policy treats as violations "images or text that indicate store performance or ranking, such as 'App of the year,' '#1,' 'Best of Play 20XX,' 'Popular,' award icons". It also says "don't use emojis, emoticons, or repeated special characters" in the title, icon or developer name — [Google Play Metadata policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9898842); background on the 2021 rollout in [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-prepare-for-new-google-metadata-policy-changes) and [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/google-clamping-down-misleading-play-store-app-listings)
- Enforcement wording varies. Some coverage says non-compliant apps "shall not be recommended or promoted"; other coverage says removal is possible — [Apptamin](https://www.apptamin.com/blog/new-aso-rules-coming-to-the-play-store); [iPhone in Canada](https://www.iphoneincanada.ca/google/google-play-store-policies/)
- Caption guidance: avoid generic promo phrases like "Best App" or "Download Now" — [AppTweak screenshots](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-optimize-your-app-screenshots)

### Inferences
- Policy risk: "best photo picker" or "best shot" used as a *feature description* (the app picks your best photo) is not a store-ranking claim. "Best photo cleaner app" or "#1 cleaner" would be. Safer phrasing: "picks your best shots". This is my reading of the policy, not a Google ruling.
- Avoid "free" in the title; it is commonly flagged under the metadata policy's promotional-language rule. I could not fetch the policy page to quote this, so verify it.
- Suggested structures (inference):
  - Title (≤30): `Cherrypic: AI Photo Cleaner` (27) / RO custom listing: `Cherrypic: Curăță Galeria` (25).
  - Short (≤80): "Swipe away duplicates & blurry shots. AI picks your best photos — on-device." (78). Count characters again after final edits.
  - Long: hook + primary keywords (2–3 lines) → 4–6 benefit sections with headers (Duplicates & similar, Best-shot AI culling, Swipe review, Organize albums, On-device editor, Privacy) → how it works (3 steps) → privacy statement (no upload) → FAQ/permissions explanation. Use keywords naturally a few times. No competitor names.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the full metadata policy text for the exact current wording on "free", calls to action and price claims.
- No 2025–2026 study quantifying how much the short description affects ranking.

## 3. Screenshots, video, feature graphic, custom listings, experiments

### Takeaway
Upload 2–8 phone screenshots (JPEG or 24-bit PNG, 320–3,840 px, 16:9 or 9:16 recommended), a required 1024×500 feature graphic, and an optional YouTube promo video. The first screenshot and the video matter most on Play. Up to 50 custom store listings can target countries, and each country can belong to only one. Store listing experiments test graphics and localized text, but results get muddied when custom listings also exist.

### Cited Findings
- Screenshots: minimum 2, maximum 8 per device type, 320–3,840 px, 16:9 or 9:16 recommended. Feature graphic 1024×500, which Google's help page says you must provide to publish. The promo video is a YouTube URL, landscape, 30 s–2 min recommended by third parties — [Google Play Console help: preview assets](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9866151?hl=en); [App Radar, updated July 2026](https://appradar.com/academy/android-app-screenshots); [AppLaunchFlow promo video 2026](https://www.applaunchflow.com/blog/google-play-promo-video-requirements-2026); [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/mastering-google-play-store-listing-requirements)
- On Google Play the first screenshot and the video preview matter most. Play favours clarity and scenarios: Android mockups, longer text, before/after shots — [ASOMobile 2025 screenshot guide](https://asomobile.net/en/blog/screenshots-for-app-store-and-google-play-in-2025-a-complete-guide/)
- Rules of thumb: captions of about 4–8 words, text no more than about 20% of the image, a panoramic design across the first three to encourage swiping, and a "5-person test" (can viewers say what the app does in a few seconds?) — [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/how-to-optimize-your-app-screenshots); [Appalize](https://www.appalize.com/blog/app-design/app-store-screenshot-design-best-practices-for-conversions); [AppScreens 2026 playbook](https://appscreens.com/blog/app-store-screenshot-optimization). The figure "90% don't scroll past the 3rd screenshot" is iOS-centric vendor data.
- Custom store listings: up to 50 per app (raised from 5 in May 2022). A country can be in only one custom listing at a time. Listings can also be targeted by keyword (search), Google Ads campaign, install state or a unique URL. Icon, category and in-app products are global; title, descriptions and graphics can vary — [Google Play Console: custom store listings](https://play.google.com/console/about/customstorelistings/); [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/google-play-store-adds-custom-store-listings/); [MobileAction glossary](https://www.mobileaction.co/glossary/what-is-custom-store-listings/); [AppTweak](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/custom-store-listings)
- Experiments: two types, default graphics and localized (text and graphics), and only one type runs at a time. Gummicube warns experiments don't separate main-listing and custom-listing traffic, so results can be skewed. MobileAction says tests are per listing. This conflicts and was unresolved for 2025–2026 — [MobileAction](https://www.mobileaction.co/?p=15869); [Gummicube](https://www.gummicube.com/blog/google-play-store-adds-custom-store-listings/); [Yellowhead](https://www.yellowhead.com/?p=4789)

### Inferences
- Screenshot plan for Cherrypic (8 frames). (1) Before/after: "1,248 photos → 312 keepers", with the brand and the core promise. (2) Swipe-to-keep UI. (3) "AI picks the best of each burst". (4) Duplicates and similar grouped. (5) Space freed counter. (6) Organize/albums. (7) On-device editor. (8) "100% on-device. Nothing uploaded." Localize captions for RO, DE, IT, ES, FR.
- Use a localized translated listing for language markets. Use one custom store listing for Romania only if RO messaging should differ from the ro-RO translation (e.g. local-pride "made in Romania"). Language translations already cover most needs.
- A promo video is probably worth it only if it shows the before/after in the first ~5 seconds. No Play-specific data on video lift was found.

### Gaps
- No Google-published data on screenshot caption or video conversion lift on Play.
- Unconfirmed whether experiments can run alongside custom listings without contamination in 2025–2026.

## 4. Benchmarks, ratings impact and early traction

### Takeaway
Google Play rates are measured per store-listing visitor, so they look higher than App Store rates. Third-party averages are about 27% (H1 2024), and one blog gives photography a 25–32% range. Since June 2026 Play Console's primary metric is click-through rate (clicks on Install/Open/Pre-register), which replaces "conversion rate". Ratings strongly affect conversion; the often-cited 3→4★ = +89% figure comes from a 2015 survey. Pre-registration is free and can include a one-time reward.

### Cited Findings
- Google Play's average conversion was 27.3% in H1 2024, against 25% on the US App Store (AppTweak data cited by Adapty and AppFollow). AppTweak's 2025 article gives a US App Store average of 8.56%, so methodologies differ — [Adapty](https://adapty.io/blog/app-store-conversion-rate/); [AppFollow](https://appfollow.io/glossary/conversion-rate); [AppTweak 2025](https://www.apptweak.com/en/aso-blog/average-app-conversion-rate-per-category-2019)
- Photography category: typical 25–32%, top tier above 40%, low below 18%. No dataset is cited — [Appalize 2026](https://www.appalize.com/ar/blog/case-studies/app-store-conversion-rate-benchmarks-by-category-2026)
- Play has no impressions metric, so its rate is measured from listing visitors and looks higher — [Gummicube](https://gummicube.com/blog/google-play-conversion-benchmarks-pitfalls-to-avoid)
- Play Console benchmarks show category quartiles, and the "50" line is the category median — [Yellowhead](https://www.yellowhead.com/blog/google-developer-console-benchmarks-update-beta/)
- From June 2026 the primary store listing metric is CTR: unique users who clicked Install, Open or Pre-register, divided by listing visitors. It replaces the legacy "Conversion rate" — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859173?hl=en); the legacy metrics remain in Statistics, per [Moburst](https://www.moburst.com/blog/google-play-console-metrics-overhaul-what-aso-teams-need-to-know/) and [Yellowhead](https://www.yellowhead.com/blog/new-google-play-console-metrics-aso/)
- Ratings: going from 3 to 4 stars is claimed to lift conversion by 89%. It is attributed variously to Apptentive (2015 survey) or AppsFlyer. 96% of survey respondents would consider a 4★ app and 50% a 3★ app. NP Digital (2024, 49 companies) gives relative conversion of 0.83 for 4★ and 0.57 for 3★. Moving from 3.9 to 4.2 is claimed to give +30% conversion — [Martech](https://martech.org/app-store-ratings-a-single-star-jump-can-mean-340-percent-more-downloads); [Alchemer](https://www.alchemer.com/resources/blog/mobile-app-ratings-and-reviews-2022-benchmarks/); [Blume](https://blume.vc/commentaries/improving-app-ratings-without-paid-reviews-the-essential-tactics); [NP Digital](https://neilpatel.com/marketing-stats/app-downloads-vs-ratings/)
- Pre-registration: up to two apps can be in pre-registration at once. One one-time reward is allowed per campaign; it must be set up before the campaign starts and cannot be edited. Failing to deliver it can lead to suspension. Google Ads pre-registration campaigns are available — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9859047); [Google Ads](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9441344)
- Downloads, download velocity and engagement are claimed to be strong Play ranking signals. This is a third-party claim that Google has not confirmed — [App Radar](https://appradar.com/academy/google-play-pre-registration-create-hype-for-apps-before-launch)

### Inferences
- For a free utility with no in-app products, a pre-registration reward isn't relevant (rewards are in-app products). Pre-registration could still collect launch-day installs if Cherrypic has an audience to drive (RO tech press such as Mobilissimo, Reddit, Product Hunt).
- Use the Play In-App Review API after a "win" moment (e.g. after freeing space or finishing a culling session) to build early ratings above 4.2. The API's existence is general knowledge, not sourced here.
- Track CTR against the Photography peer-group median in Play Console instead of third-party benchmarks.

### Gaps
- No verified Play-specific photography conversion benchmark. The Appalize figures are unsourced.
- No reliable source on how new apps get into Play's "New & updated" or editorial features. Not found.
- No data on how the minimum number of ratings needed before a star average shows affects new apps.

## 5. Play policy for photo-access apps

### Takeaway
Since early 2025, READ_MEDIA_IMAGES and READ_MEDIA_VIDEO need a Play Console declaration and are allowed only when persistent or frequent broad access is the app's core function. Gallery/photo-manager apps are "the most commonly eligible", and photo editors are named examples. A whole-library culler or cleaner fits that use case, but approval is not automatic, and a custom picker alone does not qualify. Deletion of other apps' media on Android 11+ goes through system consent dialogs (createDeleteRequest / createTrashRequest), or through the MANAGE_MEDIA special access on Android 12+. On-device-only processing does not count as "collected" in the Data safety form.

### Cited Findings
- Apps with one-time or infrequent photo needs may not use READ_MEDIA_IMAGES or READ_MEDIA_VIDEO and must use a system picker. Broad access needs a declaration and must show "a core use case that requires persistent or frequent photo/video access". Examples: photo editors, UGC, image search. "Photo/video managers and gallery apps are the most commonly eligible" — [Play Console Help: Photo and Video Permissions](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180)
- "Simply having a custom photo picking experience does not qualify" — [Android Authority](https://androidauthority.com/google-force-android-photo-picker-3491650); [9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2024/10/18/android-apps-photo-picker-policy/)
- Timeline: enforcement from 22 January 2025, with an extension request possible until 28 May 2025. One page also cites a 29 January 2025 declaration date, so the sources conflict on the exact date. Updates can be blocked without an approved declaration — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180); [Play community thread](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/thread/317596173/required-actions-to-comply-with-our-photo-video-permissions-policy-by-january-22-2025); [ProAndroidDev](https://proandroiddev.com/beware-of-read-media-images-not-just-a-feeling-of-rejection-aa1ddacaff5d)
- Remove the permissions "from all version codes within the submission", including older tracks, if you don't qualify — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14115180)
- Android docs warn that READ_MEDIA_IMAGES and READ_MEDIA_VIDEO are restricted to the Play policy use cases (answer 15800983) and recommend the photo picker — [Android Developers: media store](https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/media)
- Deletion: on Android 11+ use `createTrashRequest()` (system trash, permanently deleted after a system-defined period) or `createDeleteRequest()` (immediate permanent delete). Both show a system consent dialog. Apps that aren't the default gallery "must show a confirmation dialog… each time your app attempts to modify or delete a file". On Android 12+ (targetSdk 31+), the MANAGE_MEDIA special permission (granted via `ACTION_REQUEST_MANAGE_MEDIA` → "Media management apps" settings) lets the app trash, delete or modify without per-operation prompts. It requires declaring MANAGE_MEDIA and READ_EXTERNAL_STORAGE and showing an explanatory UI first — [Android Developers: media store](https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/media)
- Data safety: data used only on the device and never sent off it need not be declared as "collected". Ephemeral off-device processing has separate rules — [Google Play Help: data safety](https://support.google.com/googleplay/answer/11416267?hl=en); [AppMetrica docs](https://appmetrica.yandex.com/docs/en/data-security/google-data-safety)

### Inferences
- In the READ_MEDIA_IMAGES declaration, describe Cherrypic as a gallery manager and culler that must scan the whole library continuously for duplicates, similar or blurry photos and best-shot ranking. A picker can't do that because the user doesn't know which files are duplicates. Include a short demo video. Support Android 14+ partial access (READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED) gracefully. Its policy treatment was not verified here.
- Prefer `createTrashRequest` (recoverable) for bulk delete, and say so in the listing ("deleted photos go to trash, recoverable"). This reduces fear of losing photos, which a German guide flagged as a user concern ("Sicherungskopien aktivieren… Gruppen ähnlicher Fotos überprüfen") — [techzeitgeist](https://www.techzeitgeist.de/smartphone-fotos-aufraeumen-duplikate-finden-loeschen-und-automatisch-sortieren/)
- If analytics or crash SDKs (e.g. Firebase, Sentry) send data, those must be declared even if photos stay on-device. The "No data collected" badge is only accurate with no off-device transmission.
- MANAGE_MEDIA is a strong UX upgrade for a swipe-delete app. Whether Play applies any extra declaration to MANAGE_MEDIA was not verified.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full policy (answer 15800983 / 14115180) to confirm whether "cleaner" apps are explicitly listed. No public example of an approved or rejected cleaner app was found.
- The policy status of READ_MEDIA_VISUAL_USER_SELECTED and MANAGE_MEDIA under Play declarations was not verified.
- Could not verify whether Play requires any additional disclosure for on-device AI models (e.g. AI-generated content policy for editors).
